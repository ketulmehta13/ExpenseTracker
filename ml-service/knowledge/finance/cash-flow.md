# Cash flow basics

## What cash flow means
Your cash flow for a period is your income minus your expenses. Positive cash flow means money left over to save or invest; negative cash flow means you spent more than you earned and the difference came from savings or borrowing.

## Savings rate
Your savings rate is the share of income you did not spend: (income − expenses) ÷ income. Earning ₹60,000 and spending ₹48,000 gives a savings rate of 20%. Tracking it monthly shows whether your finances are improving even when income changes.

## Why spending changes month to month
Spending usually rises for a few predictable reasons: annual or quarterly bills falling in one month, festivals and travel, a new subscription or EMI, and gradual increases in one category such as food delivery. Comparing each category with the previous month or your average shows which one caused the change.

## Smoothing irregular expenses
For yearly costs such as insurance premiums or school fees, divide the amount by twelve and set that aside every month. When the bill arrives the money is already there, so one month doesn't look like a crisis.
