# Budgeting methods

## What a budget is
A budget is a plan for your income: how much goes to needs, to wants and to savings before the month starts, instead of finding out at the end where the money went. It works best when it is simple enough to follow every month and is compared with what you actually spent.

## The 50/30/20 rule
The 50/30/20 rule splits take-home income into three parts: about 50% for needs (rent, groceries, utilities, transport to work, loan EMIs), about 30% for wants (eating out, shopping, entertainment, travel) and about 20% for savings and paying off debt faster. On a take-home income of ₹50,000 that is ₹25,000 for needs, ₹15,000 for wants and ₹10,000 for savings. It is a starting point: in a high-rent city needs may take more, and then wants should shrink rather than savings.

## Zero-based budgeting
In a zero-based budget every rupee of income is given a job until income minus planned spending and saving equals zero. You list fixed costs first, then savings goals, then variable spending such as groceries and fuel, and finally wants. It takes more effort than 50/30/20 but suits irregular incomes and people who want tight control.

## The envelope method
The envelope method sets a fixed amount for each spending category at the start of the month, originally as cash in envelopes. When a category's envelope is empty you stop spending on it or move money from another envelope deliberately. In an app the envelopes are category budgets: you compare spending in each category with its limit.

## Pay yourself first
Paying yourself first means moving your savings out on payday, for example with an automatic transfer or a SIP, and budgeting only what remains. Because saving happens before spending, it does not depend on money being left over at the end of the month.

## Choosing a method
Pick 50/30/20 if you want a quick rule with little tracking, zero-based if your income varies or money is tight, and envelopes if one or two categories (often food delivery or shopping) keep running over. Whichever you choose, review it monthly against your actual spending and adjust the amounts.
