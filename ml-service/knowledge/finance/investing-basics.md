# Investing basics

## Saving first, then investing
Before investing for long-term goals, most guidance suggests having an emergency fund and paying off high-interest debt such as credit card balances. Investing money you may need within a year or two exposes you to having to sell at a bad time.

## What a SIP is
A SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) invests a fixed amount in a mutual fund at regular intervals, usually monthly. Because the amount is fixed, you buy more units when prices are low and fewer when they are high, which is called rupee cost averaging, and the habit doesn't depend on timing the market.

## Risk, return and time
Investments that can grow more, such as equity funds, also swing more in value, so they suit goals several years away. Debt funds and deposits move less and suit shorter goals. Matching the investment to when you need the money matters more than picking the best-performing product.

## Inflation
Inflation is the rise in prices over time. If your savings earn less than the inflation rate, their buying power falls even though the balance grows. This is why long-term savings are usually invested rather than kept only in a savings account.

## Not advice
This is general education, not investment advice. The app doesn't recommend specific funds, stocks or products; for personal recommendations, consult a SEBI-registered investment adviser.
