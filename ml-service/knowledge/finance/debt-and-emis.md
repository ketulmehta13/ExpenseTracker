# Debt, EMIs and credit cards

## How much EMI is too much
A common guideline is to keep all loan EMIs together below about 30 to 40% of take-home income. Above that, a small drop in income or a surprise expense can make it hard to keep up with payments.

## Credit card interest
Credit cards are interest-free only when you pay the full statement balance by the due date. Paying only the minimum amount due leaves the rest to accumulate interest at a high annual rate, usually far higher than a personal loan, plus charges on new purchases.

## Paying off several debts
The avalanche method pays the minimum on every debt and puts any extra money towards the one with the highest interest rate first, which costs the least interest overall. The snowball method puts extra money towards the smallest balance first, which gives quick wins that keep some people motivated.

## Buy now, pay later and small EMIs
Several small EMIs and buy-now-pay-later plans are easy to start and easy to forget. Treat each as a fixed monthly expense when you budget, and check the total before adding another.
