# Emergency fund

## Why you need one
An emergency fund is money kept aside only for unexpected events: a job loss, a medical bill, an urgent repair. Without one, a surprise expense usually ends up on a credit card or a personal loan, which costs interest and can start a cycle of debt.

## How much to keep
A common guideline is three to six months of essential expenses: rent, groceries, utilities, EMIs, insurance premiums and school fees, not your whole spending. People with irregular income, a single income in the family or dependants often aim for six months or more. Add up your essential spending for a typical month to find your target.

## Where to keep it
The money should be safe and available within a day or two, so it is usually kept in a separate savings account, a sweep-in fixed deposit or a liquid mutual fund rather than in shares. Keeping it separate from your everyday account makes it less tempting to spend.

## Building it step by step
Set a monthly amount, even a small one such as ₹2,000, and transfer it automatically on payday. Add windfalls such as a bonus or a tax refund. Once the target is reached, keep topping it up when your expenses grow, and refill it after you use it.
