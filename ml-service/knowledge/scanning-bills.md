# Scanning a bill: add transactions from a photo or PDF

## How to scan
Tap "Scan bill" on the Transactions page, the scan button next to Quick Add on the dashboard, or "Scan a bill" at the top of the Add form. Take a photo of the bill or upload a photo or PDF, such as a supermarket receipt, a Blinkit, Zepto or BigBasket invoice, a pharmacy bill, a restaurant bill or a fuel slip. The app reads the shop, the date, the total and the items, then shows them for you to check. Nothing is saved until you press Save.

## Splitting a bill by category
A supermarket bill often mixes groceries with household or personal care items. When the items can be grouped, "Split by category" saves one transaction per group, such as "DMart · Groceries" and "DMart · Household & personal care", each with its own category and amount. Tax, discounts and rounding are spread across the groups so they add up to the bill's total. Choose "One transaction" to save the whole bill as a single expense instead. You can change any amount or category, or untick a group, before saving.

## Where the bill is kept
When you save, the photo or PDF is stored privately with its transactions. Open it again with the receipt icon next to the transaction, or with "View bill" when editing it. Only you can see your bills, and they are deleted when you delete the transaction or your account.

## Getting a good scan
Lay the bill flat in good light with all four edges in view. Avoid shadows and folds over the total. A PDF invoice from an app is read exactly, so use it when you have one. If something is read wrongly, correct it on the review screen, or tap "Show the text that was read" to see what the app saw.

## When it can't read a bill
Very faded or crumpled thermal paper, handwritten bills and photos taken at a steep angle may not be readable. The app tells you if no text was found or if the total was a guess. Take a new photo, or add the transaction by hand.
